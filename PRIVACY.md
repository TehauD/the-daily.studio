# Privacy

**the-daily.studio collects nothing: no server keeps your data, and there is no account of its own, no analytics, no cookies and no advertising.** What you play, sing, record, type or drop in is processed in your browser and stays on your device. Nothing you make is uploaded, with one exception you choose each time: recordings you send with a song link (2.54) are kept on this site, encrypted so the site cannot open them, for 7 days (item 6 below).

That sentence is a claim about how the software behaves, so this page also says exactly what *can* travel over the network, to whom, and when. Last checked against version 2.47.0 with `test/e2e-privacy.cjs`, whose session now includes the beats, Song build, a song link and vocal tuning (see [How this is checked](#how-this-is-checked)).

## What can be sent over the network

1. **Loading the site (always).** Your browser asks Cloudflare, which hosts the site at the-daily.studio, for the page and its small files. Like any web host, Cloudflare sees your IP address, browser details and the address requested, and may keep standard access logs and aggregate traffic statistics (page requests, approximate visitor counts, countries) that the site owner can see in the Cloudflare dashboard. This project adds no analytics, cookies or tracking scripts of its own. Cloudflare's own retention is not something this project controls.
2. **MediaPipe (only after you say yes).** Hand tracking and the **Me in front (green screen)** camera look use Google's MediaPipe. The first time you use either, the page asks. If you agree, your browser downloads the MediaPipe code from `cdn.jsdelivr.net` and a small model from `storage.googleapis.com`, about 10 MB. Those two services see your IP address and browser details, as with any download. Your camera pictures are processed on your device and are never uploaded. If you say no, nothing is requested and the app uses the edges-in look or motion-only mode instead. The stage route (**Begin on stage**, **Go on stage while I sing**, **📷 Camera backdrop**) chooses the Me in front look by default, so it asks the first time; the question does not appear until the camera is on.
   Since 2.40 the page's two fonts and a few rarely used parts of the studio (the transcription engine, MIDI import, Shower mode, vocal tuning) are separate small files on the same site. Once the app has installed its offline copy, it has fetched all of them, so they work without a connection; before then, one is fetched the first time it is used. (So the site's traffic statistics cannot tell which of these parts anyone uses.) They come from the-daily.studio like the page itself, are checked against a fingerprint built into the page, and send nothing about you.
3. **Links you click.** GitHub, LinkedIn and The Daily open in a new tab only when you click them, and so does your own tip link when you press **Test the link** in the tip jar.

4. **GitHub (only if you connect it, 2.21).** In **Settings ▸ GitHub** you can keep your studio in a private repository in your own GitHub account. Only then does your browser talk to `api.github.com`, directly: it sends your settings (not this device's audio devices, levels, layout, measured latency or download consent), rig presets, stage looks and scenes, lesson notes and notebook, and songs as text (chords, words, tab, arrangement); recordings, backing tracks, pictures and videos stay on the device. The token you paste, or the one **Sign in with GitHub** gets, is kept in this browser (`fw.ghtok`), is never written to the repository and is removed by **Disconnect**. With Sign in with GitHub, this site's Worker turns GitHub's one-time code into that token and renews it; it keeps nothing and never sees your songs. If you **share looks and rigs**, the ones you choose go to a public repository in your account. Opening **Community looks and rigs** asks GitHub's search for studios that share (without signing in, unless you are connected) and reads their public files. GitHub sees your IP address and, when you are connected, your account, under its own privacy statement.

5. **Song links (2.40): nothing is sent.** **🔗 Send as a link** writes your song (chords, words, tab, the beat, tempo and key) into the link itself, after the `#`. Browsers never send the part after `#` to any server, so the song goes only where you send the link: Cloudflare sees a visit to the-daily.studio but not the song. Opening such a link reads it in the page, removes it from the address, and asks before opening it as a new song. Anyone you send it to can read the song, and so can any app the link passes through (a chat app's link preview, for example), so share it like you would the words themselves. Since 2.51 a link can also carry the name and note you type in the send window and a short history of who changed which sections and when (the names people gave, never an account). It all stays inside the link, like the song.

6. **Recordings sent with a song link (2.54): only when you tick it, encrypted, for 7 days.** In the send window, **🎙 Include my recordings** compresses the open song's takes in your browser, packs them with what each take is (name, kind, place in the song, level, pan, effects), and **encrypts the package in your browser** with a new random key. Only then is it sent to this site, which keeps it in Cloudflare's R2 storage for 7 days and then deletes it (you can **Take them back** sooner).
   - **The key** goes into the link after the `#`, so it never reaches this site, Cloudflare or anyone you didn't give the link to. Without it the stored package can't be opened by anyone, including the site's owner.
   - **To send,** you must be signed in with GitHub. The site asks GitHub which account is sending, once, with your sign-in, and keeps that account's number with the package, so abuse can be traced and the daily limit applied. It is deleted with the package. Your GitHub token is not kept.
   - **Not sent:** your backing track, stems, pictures and videos.
   - **Opening such a link** fetches the package from this site (Cloudflare sees that request, as with any visit) and unlocks it in your browser.
   - **⚑ Report** sends the site's owner the reason you type and, only if you leave the box ticked, the link's key, so the owner can listen and check. Reports are kept with the package's account number for 7 days.

7. **Drops (2.60): only when you drop a song, public by design.** **🌍 Drop it** sends the song's master (compressed in your browser), the title, the name you choose to show, your note and, if you tick it, the song as a link. Your GitHub account's number and login go with it, so the site's owner knows who sent it and the daily limit works. It's kept privately until the owner has listened. If it's approved, the audio, title, name, note and link are **public** on the-daily.studio/drops for 7 days, then deleted. If it's rejected, it's deleted. Your account number isn't shown on the public page. Reports of a drop go to the owner with its title and the account number it came from, and are kept 7 days.

8. **Soundprint links (2.61): nothing is sent.** **🔗 Copy link** writes the print (the shapes above, the title you give it, the name from your song-link settings and the day it was made) into the link after the `#`, like a song link. The sound itself is never in it and never leaves your device. Whoever opens it sees the print and hears a piece grown from it, made in their browser.

Nothing else. The page cannot contact any other site: its security policy allows only this site plus the hosts above (GitHub's API only for what you connect or open), and the test below fails if anything else is attempted.

## AI agents in your browser

The studio offers its main actions as tools for an AI agent running in your browser (WebMCP, `navigator.modelContext`; also `window.dailyStudio`): reading the song, loading a chord sheet, changing the key or tempo, editing sections, setting the band, playing and stopping, and exporting text. The tools run inside the page and send nothing anywhere. They cannot reach the microphone, the camera, recording, your other files, deleting songs or Settings. Every change an agent makes shows a 🤖 note and can be undone.

An agent is software you run, and it usually works with an AI service. When it reads your song through these tools (title, chords, lyrics), what it does with that text, including sending it to its AI provider, is up to that agent and its provider's terms, not this page. To stop agents using the studio, turn off **Settings ▸ Workflow ▸ Let AI agents in this browser use the studio**.

**Crawlers.** `robots.txt` lets search engines and AI search or answer services (which link back to the page) read the public pages, and asks crawlers that only collect pages for training AI models to stay away. That is a request: well-behaved crawlers follow it.

## What is kept, and for how long

- **By this project: nothing, except recordings you send with a song link and songs you drop.** Drops are kept privately until reviewed, then public for 7 days (item 7). Those are kept encrypted for 7 days, with the sending GitHub account's number, then deleted (item 6). Otherwise no server of this project keeps or receives your data; the site's Worker only passes GitHub's sign-in code to GitHub and the token back to you, if you use Sign in with GitHub.
- **In your browser:** the items in the table above, until you remove them.
- **By your host and the services above:** whatever their own logs keep, under their own policies. A **synced folder**, if you choose one in Settings ▸ Folder sync (Chrome and Edge), holds your studio (and, if you turn it on, a `.daily-song.zip` of each song); it is a folder on your own computer that the browser was given access to, and you can turn it off at any time. A cloud drive that folder is in keeps it under that drive's own policy.
- **Persistent storage.** The app may ask the browser to keep its library (the browser decides). Safari can still clear a site's data after about a week without a visit unless the site is added to the Home Screen; the app tells you when that applies.

## The Daily at journal.the-daily.studio

The site also hosts [The Daily](https://journal.the-daily.studio/), a separate journal app by the same author, at its own address (2.50; it was at `/journal/` before). Its entries stay in your browser. It contacts GitHub, Azure DevOps or an AI provider only if you enter your own credentials and use those features, and it sends them straight from your browser to that provider, never to this site. Its page also allows images from any `https` address, so an entry that links to a remote picture makes your browser fetch it. Because it has its own address, your browser keeps its data apart from the studio's. The studio never loads The Daily unless you open it.

**Moving from the old address.** A journal kept at `the-daily.studio/journal/` moves with **Move my journal** on that page: the page reads it in your browser and hands it to the new address in your browser, through a window it opens. Nothing is uploaded, and each page accepts the hand-over only from the other. Once it has moved, **Remove the old copy** clears it from the old address.

## Hum to Song

Humming uses your microphone (and your camera if you leave "center stage" on) only after you press the button. The recording is analysed on your device and kept in memory while you work. The saved song contains only the notes, chords and settings that were derived from it.

## Children

The app has no accounts, messaging or data collection. Songs you create stay on the device you use.

## How this is checked

- `test/e2e-privacy.cjs` opens the app in a real browser, plays a whole session (first visit, demo song, playback, Concert and its Sky poster, the Show setup console worked through tab by tab with a looks file saved, a stem split, a recording, every kind of export and the backup, and since 2.47 a beat with Song build, a song link and a tuned take) and fails if any request goes to another site, if a cookie is set, or if anything is stored outside the app's own names. It also checks that the camera and hand tracking ask before they touch jsDelivr or Google, and that "no" stays offline. After a deploy, run it against the live site: `BASE_URL=https://the-daily.studio/ node test/e2e-privacy.cjs`.
- `npm run audit` checks the shipped files, hosts and licences against [docs/LICENSING.md](docs/LICENSING.md).

If this ever changes, the change will be listed in the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) before it ships. Questions: open an issue at <https://github.com/TehauD/the-daily.studio/issues>.
